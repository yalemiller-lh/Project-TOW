# Warhammer: The Old World — Battlefield

Double-click **Play.html** to play offline. Two players share one screen. Red has three Chaos Dwarf Warrior regiments and one Blunderbuss Decimator regiment, each of 20 models, a Level 2 Daemonsmith Sorcerer, plus a Deathshrieker Rocket Launcher and three crew. Before deployment, choose green Orc Mobs (including one with warbows) or blue Empire State Troops (including one State Missile Troop regiment with crossbows, two Great Cannons, and a Level 2 Battlemage) for the opposing army. Every infantry regiment includes a musician (M), standard bearer (S), and champion (C) in the centre of its front rank; they replace three of the 20 ordinary models. Quick deploy, or place each regiment, wizard, and war machine in its 12-inch deployment zone. The top marker follows Strategy → Movement → Shooting → Combat.

The game starts in **Play against a bot** mode: you command the Chaos Dwarfs and the bot commands the opposing Orc or Empire army. Deploy your army first (or use Quick deploy, which places only your army); the bot then deploys in response to your line. Orc Mobs line up opposite your regiments, the Decimators first, on the front edge of their zone; the Empire builds a gun line where its cannons and crossbows get the most clear shots, its State Troops face your line without blocking a fire lane, and the Battlemage stands behind the middle. In the battle the bot tests Impetuous and Rally, charges, moves, casts, shoots, fires cannons, dispels your spells, and resolves combat. It pauses for your charge reactions, dispels, Shieldwall choices and pursuit choices. It uses simple distance and legal-action priorities in battle; choose **Two players** in the top bar to share the screen instead.

Both wizards are independent, unmounted 25 mm characters with two Wounds. At battle start, each randomly knows two different Battle Magic spells. On their first Strategy phase, you may exchange one of those for Hammerhand; the Daemonsmith may instead choose one of the three Lore of Hashut signatures. Click a wizard to see the spells and legal targets. Cast Enchantments and Hexes in Strategy, Arcane Urgency during Remaining Moves, Magic Missiles and Pillar of Fire in Shooting, and Assailments in Combat. Casting and Wizardly dispels use 2D6 +1 at Level 2; a Fated Dispel uses unmodified 2D6 once per turn, even when a Wizardly dispel is available. Double 1 miscasts; double 6 is irresistible. The opponent response selector lets two players choose a dispel. A visible Pillar of Fire persists, scatters each turn, damages enemies it crosses, and can be dispelled. When the Daemonsmith is within 9″ of the Deathshrieker, its re-roll is always available: after the Artillery and Scatter dice are rolled, click either die to re-roll it, or keep the roll (once per turn). Its Sorcerer’s Curse, Blackshard protection, ensorcelled weapon, Resolute and Stubborn rules are included. The Battlemage has Magical Attacks and Magic Resistance (-1). **House rule:** the published Daemonsmith lore list does not include Battle Magic; it is allowed here because this scenario requests it.

Every phase and step opens on the first unit that can act, left to right, with its orders in the side panel; click any other unit on the table to act with it instead. The panel holds one main button for that unit's action (Confirm move, Fire, Declare charge, Roll charge, Cast, Fight). The bar at the bottom only moves on: **Next unit ▸** finishes the selected unit (it holds, does not charge, or holds its fire) and selects the next; on the last unit it reads **Done · end Movement ▸** (or the phase you are in) and advances. A small **End … now** link skips the rest of a phase. Targets out of reach fold into one "why?" line under the legal ones, and a shooter starts aimed at its nearest legal target.

Movement orders: choose the pace (Normal, or March for double Movement), then one order. **Move** has a Turn (0° is straight ahead; any other angle wheels around the front corner) and a distance; **Back** and **Side** cost two inches for every inch moved; **Reform** turns the block about its centre and uses the whole move. A unit can make several steps until its movement is spent; Undo last move is under the main button. During Movement, declare charges, roll them, then move remaining units. If no legal charge is available, the charge steps are skipped. Regiments that can declare a charge remain bright; those that cannot are gray. A declared charger gains a gold outline and a gold line to its target. A charge may use one measured approach wheel and then a free wheel to align after contact. Front, flank and rear facing is fixed when the charge is declared. Chaos Dwarfs move 3 inches or march 6; Orcs and State Troops move 4 or march 8. Marching near an enemy may need a Leadership test. The dice pit is on the battlefield. Gray units have finished their phase actions.

**Charges and reactions.** Several units may charge the same enemy (into its front, flanks or rear, sharing a face when there is room), and a unit already in combat can be charged; a charger may end touching a friend that is fighting the same enemy. Once every charge is declared, each charged unit chooses **one** reaction against all its chargers: Hold; Stand & Shoot at one charger you pick (it holds against the rest, and no unit can Stand & Shoot if any of its chargers starts within that charger's Movement); or Flee, directly away from the charger with the highest Unit Strength. A unit already in combat can only Hold. Charges are rolled after all reactions. Flee pivots away from the charger and moves the sum of 2D6 inches immediately. A charger that reaches the fleeing target runs it down; otherwise it pursues for its full charge range. A missile defender must face the charger and start at least the charger's Movement value away to Stand & Shoot. The reaction fires before the charge roll, with its own −1 to hit, no long-range penalty, and the charger treated as entering weapon range. Blunderbusses ignore the Stand & Shoot penalty. A Quick Shot weapon may Stand & Shoot however close the charger is (still at −1); a Cumbersome weapon, and any war machine, cannot. Having marched in its own turn does not stop a unit Standing & Shooting. The chargers take no Panic test from these casualties. The reaction is chosen in a panel beside the charged unit on the board.

During Shooting, click a missile regiment. If no unit has a legal shot, Shooting is skipped automatically. Until the army shoots, fires a war machine or casts a spell, **← Back to Movement** reopens Remaining Moves with the turn's undo history (also from Combat when Shooting was skipped and no fight has started). Regiments in combat cannot be shot at. Its 90° front arc shades close range (the first half of the weapon's range) and far range on the table. Choose a highlighted target directly on the board or in the unit popup. The popup shows firing models, range band, movement/long-range modifiers, and the needed hit and wound rolls. Blunderbusses fire D3 shots per model and use Volley Fire from rear ranks; their weapon ignores the long-range penalty. Empire crossbows and Orc warbows fire from the front rank. Shots, wounds, armour saves, and casualties are rolled; an ordinary move gives −1 to hit. A unit that marched or is fleeing cannot shoot; a fleeing or engaged unit cannot Stand & Shoot. Hits show a brief impact flash on the target.

Click the Deathshrieker or its roster entry during the Chaos Dwarf Shooting phase. Choose **Demolition Rockets** (3″ blast, S3 with S6/AP −3 under the central hole) or **Infernal Incendiaries** (5″ blast, S3, Flaming Attacks). Both have a 12–48″ range. Select a target to place the measured circular template over its centre, then roll animated Artillery and Scatter dice. An arrow moves the template the Artillery distance; a Hit lands on the aim point. Indirect Fire is available when line of sight is blocked and a Hit still scatters by Artillery minus crew BS3. Models only partly under the template are hit on 4+. The impact circle stays on the battlefield while hits, wounds, saves, casualties, and Incendiary Panic are resolved. A Misfire uses the Black Powder Misfire table. The launcher uses its 50 × 75 mm base with three visible 25 mm crew bases. The aim point follows Bombardment and is fixed at the centre of the chosen unit. Scatter direction uses eight discrete directions in this digital prototype. The war machine is stationary; enemy regiments can charge it (see War machines in combat below). Enemy artillery can shoot it: the Deathshrieker and the Great Cannons can target each other, hitting machine and crew as one model (Toughness 6, 3 Wounds, the crew’s armour; the whole machine goes at 0 Wounds). Missile regiments cannot target war machines in this prototype. As a Weapon of War it pivots about its centre to face its target before shooting; the pivot is not a move, so targets behind it are legal. War machines are solid: no unit can move, wheel, charge, fall back, pursue or overrun through one.

When Empire is selected, deploy both Great Cannons in the blue zone. During the Empire Shooting phase, click a cannon on the board. Choose **Cannonball** (60″, S10, AP −3) and an aim point 0–10″ short of a target, then roll strike and bounce Artillery dice. The ball can hit models along its path, up to one per rank or file of each regiment. Or choose **Grapeshot** (12″, S4, AP −1) for an Artillery die of automatic hits on the selected regiment. A Misfire uses the same Black Powder table as the launcher. Each cannon has a 50 × 75 mm base and three visible crew. Cannon and rocket impacts show a short burst at the landing point and on units hit. Cannons are stationary and can be charged; the Deathshrieker can shoot them. Like the launcher, a cannon pivots to face its target before firing.

**Multi-unit combats.** Every unit joined by engagements fights as one combat. Models in base contact attack the enemy they touch; a model touching two enemy units, or a supporting model equally close to both, attacks the one the unit is focused on (choose **Focus on…** in the combat panel; the bot aims at the enemy closest to destruction). Each side adds up the wounds its units caused; it counts only its highest rank bonus (a unit engaged in its flank or rear by 10 or more enemy models has none), one standard, flank and rear bonuses once per enemy unit, Close Order for each unit with it, and Massed Infantry once for the side with more Unit Strength; a musician breaks a tie. Every unit on the losing side takes its own Break test and moves; then each winning unit that is no longer touching an enemy follows up, pursues or restrains (a broken enemy first), while one still in contact fights on next turn. During Combat, click either engaged regiment. First show each side's Initiative, then roll attacks one Initiative tier at a time. Compare combat result, roll the loser's Break test, and choose Shieldwall when available. Move the loser back, then the winner chooses follow up/pursuit or restraint. A pursuer that destroys a fleeing unit still moves its rolled distance. If attacks wipe out the enemy before the Break test, choose an overrun straight ahead or test Leadership to restrain and reform. An overrunner that does not contact a fresh enemy may test Leadership to reform afterward. Pursuit and overrun can contact a fresh enemy as a charge, then wheel to align flush with it; they fight in the next turn. Intervening units stop movement. A pursuit or an overrun (2D6 straight ahead, not Movement-based) that carries the unit off the battlefield removes it temporarily; it returns just inside that edge, facing inward and near its exit point, in its next Compulsory Moves (after charges, before Remaining Moves), and counts as having moved. A pursuer or overrunner that meets an enemy already in a combat not yet fought this phase joins that combat, fights in it as a charger and then restrains; one that meets a fresh enemy is locked with it until the next turn. No unit here has Swiftstride. Empty phases and Movement subphases advance automatically. Dead models stay dark; surviving fighting models are bright red, green or blue. A unit that breaks and survives can take a 2D6 Rally test in its next Strategy phase.

## Battle March

Choose **Battle March** under Format in the setup panel. The classic full-size game is unchanged.

- **Armies.** 400–750 points (750 by default), from costed rosters: Chaos Dwarfs from Renegades 2.0 (newrecruit catalogue revision 10) and the Empire of Man (revision 136), on game system revision 188. Every roster is checked against the Battle March muster rules: an eligible General, two non-character units, the 25% character, 35% Core, 30% Special and 25% Rare/Mercenary single-entry ceilings (never rounded up), Unit Strength 20, and one restricted "0–X per 1,000 points" choice. Illegal rosters list specific errors. Your 500-point Chaos Dwarf and Empire lists are included. K’daai Fireborn are never offered (your preference, not a rule). The Orc & Goblin army is not playable yet: no Orc character is modelled to be the General, and warbows have no cost in the source data.
- **Battlefield.** 48″ × 36″, or 44″ × 30″ at 600 points or fewer. The default is 500 points on 44″ × 30″ with Pitched Battle zones 7.5″ deep at opposite long edges.
- **Deployment maps.** All six maps, built from the official diagrams' measurements (boundaries at fixed distances from the centre lines, so zones follow the battlefield): **Pitched Battle** — long-edge zones, each boundary 7.5″ from the centre line (7.5″ deep on 44″ × 30″, 10.5″ on 48″ × 36″); **Close Encounter** — opposite quarters outside a 15″-diameter circle at the centre; **Opposed Flanks** — opposite triangles along the long edges, leaving an 18″ gap along each side edge; **Meeting Engagement** — the 15″ central gap, each zone stopping 11″ short of opposite side edges; **Mountain Pass** — zones on the short edges, 11″ either side of the centre line (armies face each other along the table); **Outflank** — opposite triangles on the short edges with a 22″ diagonal strip along the top and bottom edges. Red takes the bottom zone (the left one in Mountain Pass); which corner or side each army takes on the asymmetric maps is a mirror choice the measurements leave open, and the triangles are read as running corner to corner. A labelled custom long-edge preset (6–14″ deep) remains. Every unit's whole footprint must fit inside its zone.
- **Setup.** Objectives (a D6, or chosen) → deployment roll-off, where the winner chooses who deploys first → one unit at a time, alternating; a placed unit can be adjusted until its placement is confirmed → first-turn roll-off, where the winner chooses who goes first. The side panel walks through each step; Quick deploy places your units as your turns come.
- **Objectives.** D6 1–2 two treasure troves, 3–4 three, 5–6 one strategic landmark. Troves are 40 mm, 3″ clear of terrain, and start in an even custom layout (not an official diagram); drag them before the first unit deploys. The landmark is 100 mm, at the centre, impassable (every move, charge, flee and pursuit respects it) and blocks line of sight. Its property is rolled before deployment: 1–2 Magic Resistance (−2), 3–4 Frenzy, 5–6 Stubborn. The unit holding it gains the property until the end of the next turn; it is shown on the unit's label and tooltip, separate from its own rules.
- **Control.** At the end of every player turn, for both armies: the closest eligible unit controls each objective (within 3″ measured between footprints, current Unit Strength 5+, not fleeing). If two are equally close, the higher Unit Strength controls; if still tied, it is contested. Each trove scores 10 VP and the landmark 25 VP. The board shows each objective's footprint, its 3″ control ring and who holds it now.
- **Victory points.** Destroyed or fled off the battlefield: the unit's full points cost. Fleeing at the end, or at 25% or less of its starting Unit Strength (remaining Wounds for single models): half, rounded up, never both. The enemy General slain, fled or fleeing +50, each captured standard +25, a Battle Standard Bearer +25. The VP panel shows committed VP (objectives, raids, standards) apart from provisional casualty VP, with an itemised ledger.
- **The end.** After the second player's turn in round five the battle is scored once, the final result opens, and no more actions are possible. **End battle** in the top bar lets a player concede, or the players stop early (an agreed time limit) and score the battle as it stands. The win/draw rule for Battle March is not confirmed; the game uses the core rulebook's (win by 100 VP, crushing victory at double) and shows both exact totals.
- **Optional rules** start switched off. **Raid & Burn** is available: a unit of Unit Strength 5+ that moves into base contact with a trove in Remaining Moves can start burning it (it cannot shoot, and casts only Combat or Self spells); at its next Start of Turn the trove burns for 30 VP if the unit is still in contact, Unit Strength 5+, not engaged and not fleeing. Baggage Carts, Random Happenings, Battle March magic items, secret objectives, narrative scenarios and event rules are listed but stay unavailable until their rules are supplied.
- **The bot** deploys one unit at a time in answer to yours and makes its own roll-off choices. In battle it sends its units to claim objectives (the landmark first) when they can reach them, keeps its characters a few inches behind its battle line (its General is worth 50 VP to you), does not charge regiments with characters, holds objectives in the last round, and burns a trove when that scores more than holding it. It uses the same legality, measurement and scoring functions as you.

## Magic and dispelling

Spells are cast in their phase (hexes and enchantments in Strategy, conveyance in Remaining Moves, magic missiles and vortices in Shooting, assailments in Combat); a Level 2 Wizard attempts up to two different spells per turn, and failed attempts count. The casting roll is 2D6 + half the level (rounded up), rolled beside the Wizard. A natural double 6 is a Perfect Invocation that cannot be dispelled; a natural double 1 miscasts. Once a spell is cast, the defending player chooses the dispel: dashed lines join the caster to every enemy Wizard that can dispel (within 18″, not fleeing, and not engaged unless the spell targets it). Click a Wizard or its button for a Wizardly Dispel (2D6 + half level), choose the once-per-turn Fated Dispel (unmodified 2D6, no Wizard or range needed, allowed even when a Wizard could dispel), or let it through. A dispel must beat the casting result (ties fail), double 6 always dispels, and double 1 always fails; on a Wizardly Dispel it also makes that Wizard miscast (Outclassed in the Art). The dispel dice roll beside the dispelling Wizard. A Pillar of Fire that remains in play can be dispelled in the opposing player's next Strategy phase by beating its printed 9+. Against a bot, the bot makes its own dispel choices and waits for yours.

## War machines in combat

The Deathshrieker and the Great Cannons can be charged like any regiment, from any side, and may only Hold. The machine and its crew form a single model; the crew bases are tokens. In combat the model uses its crew profile (Chaos Dwarf crew WS3 S3 T4 I2 Ld9; Empire crew WS3 S3 T3 I3 Ld7) with one Attack per surviving crew token and no armour save. Each unsaved wound removes a crew token, and the last one destroys the machine. War machines have no flanks or rear, no ranks and no Shieldwall. A war machine never moves: if it breaks, the crew abandon it and it is destroyed; after Fall Back or Give Ground it stays in the combat. A winning war machine cannot follow up, pursue or overrun. An engaged war machine cannot fire. Crew armour is not modelled.

## Measurements and rules

- Table: 72 × 48 inches (6 × 4 feet). Deployment zones: 12 inches deep. The SVG scales the board to the available screen; measurements remain tabletop inches.
- Each regiment: 5 files × 4 ranks. Chaos Dwarf and State Troop bases are 25 × 25 mm; their blocks measure 125 × 100 mm. Orc bases are 30 × 30 mm; their blocks measure 150 × 120 mm.
- Renegades 2.0 Chaos Dwarf Warriors: M3 WS4 BS3 S3 T4 W1 I2 A1 Ld9, hand weapons, heavy armour and shields. Base armour 4+; Parry improves the shield save to 3+ in these close combats.
- Orc Mob (Orc Boy): M4 WS3 BS3 S3 T4 W1 I3 A1 Ld6, hand weapons and light armour (6+). A 3-inch charge gives Furious Charge; Choppas reroll 1s to wound and add Armour Piercing on a charge. Warband adds rank bonus to Leadership where permitted and automatically rerolls a failed charge range. An Orc Mob able to charge tests Impetuous; failure forces a charge.
- Empire State Troops (State Trooper): M4 WS3 BS3 S3 T3 W1 I3 A1 Ld7, hand weapons, light armour and the optional shield. Base armour 5+; Parry improves it to 4+ in combat.
- Blunderbuss Decimators: M3 WS3 BS3 S3 T4 W1 I2 A1 Ld9, heavy armour, 12-inch blunderbuss (S3, AP −1, Multiple Shots D3, Volley Fire). The 5 × 4 regiment is on 25 mm bases.
- Deathshrieker Rocket Launcher: T6 W3, 50 × 75 mm base, with Chaos Dwarf Crew (M3 WS3 BS3 S3 T4 W3 I2 A3 Ld9) shown on three adjacent 25 × 25 mm bases. Demolition Rockets use the 3″ template; Infernal Incendiaries use the 5″ template.
- Empire Great Cannons: two stationary T6 W3 machines on 50 × 75 mm bases, each with three visible crew. Cannonball reaches 60″ at S10/AP −3; grapeshot reaches 12″ at S4/AP −1.
- State Missile Troops: M4 WS3 BS3 S3 T3 W1 I3 A1 Ld7, crossbows (30 inches, S4). Their Sergeant has BS4. Orc Mob with warbows: M4 WS3 BS3 S3 T4 W1 I3 A1 Ld6, warbows (24 inches, S3), light armour and 30 mm bases.
- Fighting rank and Press of Battle models attack. Hit, wound and save targets use the opposing profiles. Charging gives an Initiative bonus. Combat result includes unsaved wounds, ranks, Close Order, Massed Infantry and flank/rear bonuses. A losing unit takes a 2D6 Break test with the combat-result difference.
- Full command: M–S–C occupy the middle three front-rank squares, with the standard at the centre. The Orc Boss has Leadership 7 and 2 Attacks; the State Missile Troop Sergeant has BS4 and 1 Attack. Other champions have 2 Attacks. A living standard adds +1 combat result. If otherwise tied, a sole musician adds +1 combat result. A living musician adds +1 Leadership to rally tests and march tests made within 8 inches of an enemy, up to Leadership 10. Casualties are removed physically from the right-hand end of the rear rank, so the survivors stay together and the fighting rank stays full; the musician, then the standard bearer, then the champion are removed only when no ordinary warriors remain. In combat, casualties a unit suffers before its Initiative step still count against its first fighting rank, then its second: the warriors who step forward cannot attack that phase. Blows at the same Initiative are simultaneous. There are no characters or challenges, so attacks are never directed at a champion.
- If applicable, the loser may choose Shieldwall to turn Fall Back in Good Order into Give Ground. Resolute reduces flee/pursuit distance by one inch. Rally uses 2D6 at or below Leadership 9; a rallied unit cannot charge that turn.

This is a small tabletop prototype. The charge planner supports one measured approach wheel plus a free alignment wheel, for front, flank and rear charges. Terrain other than the Battle March landmark, character joining and challenges, magical command upgrades, victory conditions in the classic game, saving a game, charge redirection, charges that would have to be declared against two units at once, and full compulsory fleeing movement are not simulated. Flee moves (Flee reactions, broken units, and Panic from spells or Incendiaries) go straight ahead through other units. Each friendly unit passed through takes a Panic test and, if it fails, flees directly away from the fleeing unit, which can chain. Every model whose path crossed an enemy unit takes a Peril test and loses a wound on 1–3. A flee that would end on or within 1″ of an enemy continues until it is 1″ beyond; one that would end on a friend continues until clear by 1″, because this prototype keeps 1″ between all units. Engaged or already fleeing friends and war machines do not test Panic. Panic from nearby units fleeing or being destroyed, and compulsory flee moves in later turns, are not simulated. Shooting does not yet include panic from missile casualties, cover terrain, or unusual weapon options. Blocks keep their full 5 × 4 footprint as they lose models, so casualties never break base contact and no closing-the-gap move is needed. Regiments keep their 5 × 4 footprint. The game resets on reload. Renegades is a community army list, separate from the official Games Workshop legacy list.

The post-combat choice order follows this prototype's requested play flow: the loser moves before the winner chooses. In the published Old World rules, the winner chooses whether to follow up or pursue before the loser moves. A fresh enemy contacted by pursuit fights in the next turn and counts the pursuer as charging. Pursuers that leave the battlefield return near their exit point in their next Movement phase, facing inward and counting as moved. The charge planner searches in 5-degree wheel steps, so some otherwise legal tabletop approaches may still need manual repositioning in an earlier turn.

## Files and checks

- `Play.html`: bundled, standalone game.
- `dist/`: editable HTML, CSS and JavaScript source for a local server.
- `dist/ai.mjs`: optional opponent decisions and turn sequencing.
- `dist/formats.mjs`: game formats (classic and Battle March), battlefields, deployment maps and optional rules.
- `dist/armies.mjs`: costed army entries, the 500-point lists and Battle March roster validation.
- `dist/battlemarch.mjs`: objectives, objective control, Raid & Burn, victory points and the result.
- Live site: https://yalemiller-lh.github.io/Project-TOW/ — GitHub Pages deploys the `main` branch root on every push; `index.html` opens `Play.html`, and `.nojekyll` serves files as-is. Rebuild and commit `Play.html` with every `dist/` change.
- `.github/workflows/tests.yml`: runs every test file on each push and pull request, and checks that `Play.html` matches `dist/`.
- `package-standalone.mjs`: rebuilds `Play.html` from `dist/`.
- `test-*.mjs`: movement, charges, model eligibility, movement budget, combat, cannon, bot, and presentation checks. Run each with Node.js. `test-battlemarch.mjs` covers objectives, control ties, scoring once per turn end, landmark effect expiry, casualty VP, alternating deployment and both roll-offs, Raid & Burn, ending the battle, and the bot's deployment and objective play; `test-playthrough.mjs` plays whole seeded games (`SEEDS=n` for more).
